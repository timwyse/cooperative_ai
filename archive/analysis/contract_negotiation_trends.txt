5. MODEL-SPECIFIC BEHAVIORAL PATTERNS
================================================================================

FOUR_1-FOUR_1 (GPT-4.1):
- Most efficient negotiator (6.0 avg messages).
- In Needy_Player_Blue finishing, the dominant pattern is (0, 0) - both
  players agree to exchange no points (16 out of 41 times). When one
  player is independent, FOUR_1 often concludes "no cooperation needed"
  rather than extracting payment. This is the LEAST extractive model.
- However, when it does extract, it goes for full (0, 20) 8 times.
- Shows a binary pattern: either no deal or full extraction.

HAIKU_4_5-HAIKU_4_5 (Haiku 4.5):
- Most verbose negotiator (9.9 avg messages).
- Engages in thorough analytical verification of claims.
- Produces the most balanced outcomes in finishing condition (~10/10 splits).
- Can be manipulated through persistent framing (see Example 1).
- Attempted deception was observed (deliberately reversing contract terms)
  but was caught (see Example 8).
- Highest agreement rate (99%).

LLAMA_MAVERICK-LLAMA_MAVERICK:
- Most cooperative model overall. In NP finishing, (10,10) was the most
  common outcome (9 times), (8,8) next (5 times).
- Creates the largest strict contracts (6.1 items in NP) - willing to
  do extensive chip exchanges.
- Average point transfers in finishing are the most balanced among models.
- Shows genuine "mutual benefit" reasoning rather than exploitation.

LLAMA_SCOUT-LLAMA_SCOUT:
- Moderate negotiator with 99% agreement rate.
- Produces relatively small contracts (3.2-3.5 items in strict).
- In finishing condition, tends toward lower point transfers (avg 3.9/4.6
  in NP) - models negotiate conservatively.
- Player 0 consistently gets slightly better deals across conditions.

QWEN_3_235B-QWEN_3_235B:
- THE MOST EXTRACTIVE MODEL. In NP finishing, 23 out of 35 agreements
  were (0, 20) - maximum extraction by the self-sufficient player.
- Even in Mutual_Dependency finishing, (0, 20) occurred 5 times.
- When both need each other, can achieve fair splits (10,10) x7.
- Shows a clear strategic pattern: if I don't need you, I take everything.
- Occasional role confusion (mixing up which player has which chips)
  but still reaches extractive outcomes.

QWEN_3_30B-QWEN_3_30B:
- Second most extractive model. (0, 20) was the dominant pattern in both
  MD finishing (11 out of 22) and NP finishing (18 out of 40).
- Highest failure rate in strict condition (6 failures in NP strict).
- Can be both confused and brutally selfish - sometimes makes analytical
  errors but still refuses to cooperate.
- Average P1 gives 16.6 in MD finishing - the highest extraction rate.


================================================================================
6. BOARD TYPE DIFFERENCES
================================================================================

Mutual_Dependency:
- Agreement rate: 230/233 (99%) - very high.
- Both players need each other's chips, creating symmetric leverage.
- Negotiations tend to be more balanced (avg P0 gives 1.58, P1 gives 1.62
  in strict; more varied in finishing).
- Models that try to exploit in MD often face pushback since both players
  can threaten to walk away.

Needy_Player_Blue:
- Agreement rate: 422/443 (95%) - slightly lower.
- Player 0 (Red-rich) is typically self-sufficient.
- Player 1 (Blue-rich) often cannot reach the goal without Red chips.
- This creates a clear power asymmetry that gets exploited, especially
  by QWEN models.
- Failures are concentrated here (13 out of 24 total failures).
- The needy player often accepts heavily lopsided deals because the
  alternative is scoring 0.


================================================================================
7. CONDITION DIFFERENCES (STRICT vs FINISHING)
================================================================================

Strict condition (tile-level exchanges):
- Negotiations focus on specific path analysis and tile-by-tile coverage.
- Models engage in more analytical reasoning about paths, chip costs,
  and alternative routes.
- Contracts tend to be more "fair" because both players must physically
  cover tiles for each other - there's a natural reciprocity.
- Average P0 gives 2.06, P1 gives 2.41 - relatively balanced.

Finishing condition (point transfers):
- Negotiations become more abstract game-theoretic arguments.
- The self-sufficient player has enormous leverage: "I don't need you,
  but you need me, so give me everything."
- This leads to extreme outcomes: either (0, 0) when both are independent,
  or (0, 20) when one player is desperate.
- Average P0 gives 1.00, P1 gives 1.00 (in chip items) but the POINT
  transfers are highly variable (0 to 20).
- Models that are cooperative (MAVERICK) tend toward 10/10 splits.
- Models that are extractive (QWEN) tend toward 0/20 splits.


================================================================================
8. FAILED NEGOTIATIONS
================================================================================

Total failures: 24 out of 676 (3.6%)

By model:
- FOUR_1: 4 failures (all NP strict)
- HAIKU: 1 failure (NP finishing)
- MAVERICK: 5 failures (2 strict, 3 finishing)
- SCOUT: 1 failure (NP finishing)
- QWEN_235B: 7 failures (1 MD strict, 1 MD finishing, 5 NP finishing)
- QWEN_30B: 6 failures (all NP strict)

Common failure patterns:
1. Both players independent, refuse to trade: Both correctly calculate they
   don't need help, but the system generates a contract anyway that neither
   wants to sign.
2. Self-sufficient player refuses to help: Player 0 can go solo and sees no
   benefit in cooperation, even at zero cost. "I do not care" attitude.
3. Contract terms don't match conversation: Players agree in conversation but
   the judge-generated contract doesn't match what was discussed.
4. Role confusion: QWEN models sometimes confuse which player they are,
   leading to incoherent proposals.


================================================================================
9. COMMON NEGOTIATION TACTICS OBSERVED
================================================================================

1. ANCHORING: Opening with extreme demands (16/4, 0/20) to shift the
   negotiation range. Most effective for QWEN models.

2. PATH ANALYSIS: Detailed tile-by-tile calculations to prove need (or
   lack thereof). Used by all models but especially FOUR_1 and HAIKU.

3. POWER ASYMMETRY EXPLOITATION: "I can reach my goal without you, but you
   cannot reach yours without me." The dominant tactic in Needy_Player_Blue.

4. INCREMENTAL CONCESSIONS: Starting far apart and moving toward each other
   in small steps. Characteristic of MAVERICK and HAIKU.

5. TAKE-IT-OR-LEAVE-IT: Stating a demand and saying "agree" without waiting
   for counter-proposals. Very common with QWEN models.

6. DECEPTION: Attempting to reverse contract terms or misrepresent the
   situation. Observed with HAIKU (caught) and QWEN_3_30B (confused).

7. MUTUAL VERIFICATION: Both players independently computing paths and
   checking each other's math. HAIKU's signature tactic.

8. NO-DEAL WALK-AWAY: "No cooperation is necessary. I will proceed alone."
   Used by FOUR_1 and QWEN when they have self-sufficient paths.


================================================================================
10. OVERALL SUMMARY
================================================================================

The most significant finding is the stark behavioral difference between
model families:

- COOPERATIVE SPECTRUM: MAVERICK > HAIKU > SCOUT > FOUR_1 > QWEN_30B > QWEN_235B

MAVERICK negotiates the most fairly, consistently achieving near-equal splits
even when it has bargaining leverage. QWEN_235B is the most extractive, with
a dominant strategy of maximum extraction (0/20) whenever it has the upper hand.

The two contract conditions create fundamentally different negotiation dynamics:
- STRICT contracts enforce natural reciprocity (both must cover tiles).
- FINISHING contracts enable extreme extraction (one player can demand all points).

The Needy_Player_Blue board consistently produces the most dramatic negotiations,
as it creates a clear power asymmetry that models handle very differently -
from generous cooperation (MAVERICK) to ruthless exploitation (QWEN).
